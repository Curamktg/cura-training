The Manual
The complete Cura pitch system — tap any chapter to read.
01
The Cura Standard
Who we are and what we stand for
▾

Cura Marketing is not just a sales team — it is a culture of excellence. Every rep who puts on this name represents something bigger than a paycheck. We are building a legacy.

Our Core Values

  • We show up every day with energy and intention
  • We are coachable — ego is the enemy of growth
  • We hold ourselves to a higher standard than average
  • We compete hard, celebrate each other, and never make excuses

The reps who succeed here understand that sales is a skill — and like any skill, it compounds. The work you put in on day 10 pays off on day 60.

02
The Product
Know what you're selling inside out
▾

We sell pest control services. But that is not how you think about it. You are selling peace of mind, protection of the home, and a problem solved before it becomes a headache.

What's Included

  • Quarterly service visits by licensed technicians
  • Interior and exterior treatment
  • Spider, ant, roach, rodent, and general pest coverage
  • Satisfaction guarantee — we come back if bugs return

Why Customers Buy

  • They've seen or had bugs recently
  • They have kids or pets and want a clean home
  • They already pay for pest control and want to compare
  • Their neighbor just signed up (social proof is powerful)
03
The Approach
From driveway to doorstep
▾

The sale begins before you say a word. How you walk up the driveway, how you carry yourself, the look on your face — it all signals to the customer whether they should trust you.

The Approach Formula

  • Walk with purpose — no dragging feet, no looking at your phone
  • Smile as you approach — warmth opens doors, literally
  • Stand slightly to the side of the door — not directly in front (less threatening)
  • Make eye contact immediately when the door opens

First 3 Seconds

You have 3 seconds to create a positive first impression. Your opener must be conversational, confident, and natural — not robotic. Practice it until it sounds like you just thought of it.

04
The Opener
The words that earn you 60 more seconds
▾

Your opener has one job: earn the next 60 seconds. It is not designed to close — it is designed to open a conversation. If you make it too salesy, the door closes.

Opener Structure

  • Establish why you're in the neighborhood (social proof)
  • Acknowledge them as a homeowner (respect their space)
  • Transition into the reason for the visit naturally

Example Openers

  • "Hey, how's it going? I'm actually working with a few of your neighbors on [street] today..."
  • "Hey, quick question — do you guys currently have anything for pest control?"
  • "I'm not sure if your neighbor mentioned it, but we're out here this week setting up service..."
05
Building Value
Make them want it before they hear the price
▾

Price is never the problem. Perceived value is the problem. If the customer feels like the service is worth $300, then $150 is a deal. Your job is to get them to that $300 feeling before you ever say a number.

Value Building Steps

  • Paint the problem — what pests cost homeowners in damage
  • Describe the service in detail — more detail = more value perception
  • Use social proof — "we've treated 12 homes on this street"
  • Anchor high before revealing price
06
Objection Handling
Every no is a step toward yes
▾

Objections are not rejections — they are questions in disguise. When someone says "I need to think about it," they are really saying "I'm not convinced yet." Your job is to find out what's missing.

The Big 4 Objections

  • "I need to talk to my spouse" — get them on the phone, or set a callback
  • "I already have a service" — ask who, then differentiate
  • "It's too expensive" — revisit value, offer a smaller entry point
  • "I need to think about it" — find the real objection underneath

The Golden Rule

Never argue. Agree first, then redirect. "I totally get that — most people say the same thing at first. What specifically had you concerned?"

07
The Close
Asking for the sale with confidence
▾

The close is the natural conclusion of a well-run pitch. If you've built value correctly, the close should feel like you're doing the customer a favor — not pressuring them.

Closing Principles

  • Assume the sale — "Let me go ahead and get you set up..."
  • Be direct — stop talking after you ask for the sale
  • Use urgency when real — timing, neighborhood pricing, availability
  • If they hesitate, go back to the objection, not the pitch

Close Language

  • "Let's get you locked in today — what's the best address for service?"
  • "Go ahead and grab your ID for me and we'll get you taken care of."
  • "I'll put you down — did you want to start with the interior as well?"
08
Mindset & Daily Habits
The mental edge that separates top reps
▾

Sales is 80% mental. The rep who can shake off a rough hour and reset faster than everyone else will always outsell the rep who lets a bad door ruin their day.

Daily Non-Negotiables

  • Review your pitch and goals before you leave in the morning
  • Track your numbers — doors knocked, pitches given, accounts sold
  • Debrief yourself after each day — what worked, what didn't
  • Compete with yourself first, the leaderboard second

When You Hit a Wall

  • Take 60 seconds, reset, and walk to the next door with a clean slate
  • Call your manager or a top rep — talk through what's happening
  • Remember: every no moves you statistically closer to a yes
